# 104 BoT-SORT integration — 2026-10-23

The deployed final case remains `04_09_body_tracking`: YOLO26n-seg TensorRT,
BoT-SORT, native-mask 16×16 sparse depth, existing C++ Body Tracking controller
and chassis arbitration. Validation runs with `enable_motion=false`.

## Changes

- Detector candidate cutoff 0.1; high-score association and new tracks 0.7.
  The previous 0.7 prefilter made the configured low-score association ineffective.
- BoT-SORT sparse optical flow; compensation image downscaled 4×, at most
  200 corners, 4-pixel spacing. ReID disabled; no additional neural model.
- Follow identity stays distinct from raw `tracker_id`. For gaps up to 2 seconds,
  match camera-compensated predicted position, box scale, a small torso HSV
  histogram and recent depth where available. Similar ambiguous candidates are
  left unmatched. A missing target is not replaced with a fabricated detection.
- Status exposes `tracker_backend`, `tracker_ms`, `gmc_ms`,
  `detector_candidate_threshold`, `follow_identity_id`, `detector_track_id`,
  `identity_rebindings` and per-track `tracker_id`.
- Existing native identity retention changed from 30 to 90 processed frames.
  Existing missing-frame movement cancellation is unchanged.
- Active camera is Orbbec Gemini Pro, SDK 1.10.35, not the earlier Hikrobot.
  Property 189 (native color AE ceiling) reports unsupported. RGB uses fixed
  exposure 50 SDK units (5 ms), gain 64; these settings are in
  `gemini_pro_104.launch.py`. Automatic exposure is disabled, rather than claiming
  to enforce an unsupported native AE ceiling. Depth settings are unchanged.

## Verification

- 82 Python tests pass on 104, including actual installed BoT-SORT low-score
  continuation/new-track rejection, camera-shift tracking and identity continuity
  after raw-ID breaks; appearance/depth mismatch and ambiguity cases covered.
- Existing human RGB-D recording replayed on 104: 180 total frames, 20 warmup,
  160 measured. All 160 measured frames retained the followed person and valid
  sparse depth. No GMC warnings. No new gestures or recording requested.
- Replay mean perception 25.10 ms, identity 0.53 ms, depth 2.56 ms, GMC 8.89 ms.
  This is recorded-frame processing timing; it excludes JPEG/Fox transport and
  must not be represented as end-to-end FPS or a moving-robot acceptance result.
- Live empty-scene preview: 305 status frames and 301 JPEGs received by a ROS
  validator; about 16.4 FPS. Separate Windows WebSocket validation received 211
  JPEGs at 17.69 FPS and 210 status messages at 17.72 FPS. Fox desktop connected
  without reported problems and rendered the live result image.
- RGB exposure 50 and gain 64 read back; 15 live RGB frames received. Mean pixel
  brightness rose from about 20 at gain 32 to 26 at gain 64. The room remains
  dim; this does not establish reliable human recognition in low illumination.
- Vehicle motion during blur, vibration and multi-person crossing has not been
  physically accepted. Position/HSV matching is not learned person ReID and
  cannot guarantee identity when similarly dressed people cross.
